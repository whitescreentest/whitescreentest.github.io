What is a white screen test?

A white screen test fills your whole display with one solid color. With nothing else on screen, every flaw shows up: a dead pixel becomes a dark dot, backlight bleed shows as brighter patches at the edges, and uneven brightness is easy to spot. It is the simplest display test there is — and it needs no app.

⚪ White screen

The classic test. A pure white screen shows dead pixels, dust under the glass and uneven brightness better than any other color.

⬛ Black screen

A black screen reveals backlight bleed and stuck pixels that glow when they should be off — the most common issue on new TVs and monitors.

🟥 Red, green & blue

Each primary color lights up only one set of subpixels. A dot that stays dark on red but not on green tells you exactly which subpixel is stuck.

How to test your screen in 4 steps

  1. Pick a color

    Start with white. Click a color card below the preview, or choose from the list on the left.

  2. Go full screen

    Click the preview or the expand button (or press F). The color fills the whole display so nothing distracts from the test.

  3. Look closely

    Check the whole screen for dark dots, bright spots, lines or patches that are lighter or darker than the rest.

  4. Repeat with other colors

    Switch to black, then red, green and blue. Some faults only appear on one color, so test them all before you decide.

Frequently asked questions

What is a white screen used for?

Mostly to test a display: it shows dead pixels, backlight bleed and uneven brightness. People also use it as a light for video calls, to check for dust while cleaning a screen, or as a plain color background.

How do I make my screen completely white?

Click the White Screen card above, then click the preview (or press F) to go full screen. The whole display turns white. Press Esc to exit.

Can a white screen fix a stuck pixel?

Sometimes. A stuck pixel is a pixel frozen on one color; flashing bright colors can occasionally unstick it. A truly dead pixel (always black) cannot be fixed by any screen. Our color screens can help you test which one it is.

Is it safe for my screen?

Yes. Showing a solid color is the most ordinary thing a screen does — it is no different from looking at a white web page. It cannot damage a healthy display.

Does it work on phones and TVs too?

Yes. Open this page on any device with a browser — phone, tablet, laptop, monitor or smart TV — pick a color and go full screen.

What is backlight bleed?

On LCD screens, light from behind the panel can leak around the edges, showing as bright patches on a black screen. A little bleed is normal; large bright areas on a new screen are worth returning.

Tips for a good screen test

🌑 Test in a dark room

Backlight bleed and faint stuck pixels are much easier to see when the room is dark.

🔆 Turn brightness up

Set the screen to full brightness for the test, so faults have nowhere to hide.

🔍 Get close

Dead pixels are tiny. Look at the screen from close up, section by section, instead of from across the room.

🎨 Try every color

Test white, black, red, green and blue. A pixel can be dead in one color and fine in the others.

🧽 Clean first

Wipe the screen before testing. A speck of dust looks just like a dead pixel on a white screen.

📱 Test new devices early

Check a new phone, monitor or TV within the return window, so a faulty panel can still be exchanged.
